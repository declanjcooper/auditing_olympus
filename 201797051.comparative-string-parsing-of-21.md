## **Objective**

To isolate the divergence between structural rule verification and semantic proximity parsing when evaluating document compliance.

**Baseline Reference Source:** FDA 21 CFR Part 11.50(a)

### I. The Canonical Reference Standard

Federal rules state that signed electronic records must contain associated metadata that explicitly and clearly maps three specific data strings:

1. **The printed name of the signer**
2. **The date and time when the signature was executed**
3. **The meaning associated with the signature** (such as review, approval, responsibility, or authorship)

### II. The Incoming Signal (The Ingested Payload)

*This is a generated standard market statement representing a typical vendor’s description of digital signature compliance:*

> “The platform locks down approvals using an integrated signature pad. Every single submission binds the signer’s digital identity to a secure cryptographic certificate to prevent file alteration or forgery.”

### IV. Output Breakdown

#### Engine A: Structural Parser (Deterministic Rules)

* **Execution State:** **FALSE (Incomplete Structure)**
* **Logic Rationale:** Ingestion halted. The document buffer was parsed for explicit parameters. The character sequences `['printed name', 'date and time', 'meaning']` were not verified within the payload bounds.
* **Compute Metric:** Low Energy [O(1) memory address sequence check].

#### Engine B: Semantic Engine (Probabilistic Vector Mapping)

* **Execution State:** **TRUE (Contextual Proximity Confirmed)**
* **Logic Rationale:** Ingest allowed. Multi-matrix calculations detected high vector proximity based on cluster tokens: `['pad', 'certificate', 'identity', 'signed']`. The engine maps these tokens conceptually to fulfill the intent of electronic record validation.
* **Compute Metric:** High Energy (Dense multi-matrix traversal over parameter weights).

### V. Systems Observation

The outputs displayed here represent the mechanical limits of two distinct processing architectures. One requires explicit, verified nodes to establish state. The other maps relational tokens to establish context.

The resulting delta (the discrepancy between an ‘incomplete’ structural evaluation and a ‘confirmed’ contextual mapping) is the observable outcome we want to understand when thinking of use cases.
